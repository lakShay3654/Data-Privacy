# Practical 1: Data Privacy Audit of Coursera

## Aim - To conduct a data privacy audit of the Coursera website and identify how user data is collected, used, stored, and protected.

### Website Selected

#### Organization: Coursera, Inc.
#### Website: Coursera (https://www.coursera.org)
#### Privacy Policy: Coursera Privacy Notice (https://www.coursera.org/about/privacy)

### Requirements

Internet connection
Web browser
Coursera account (optional)

### Procedure

• Visit the Coursera website.

• Open its Privacy Notice.

• Identify the types of personal information collected.

• Check how the information is used.

• Examine cookies and tracking technologies.

• Check third-party services and data sharing.

• Examine available privacy and security controls.

• Identify possible privacy risks.

• Suggest measures to improve privacy.

### Observations:-
| Privacy Aspect         | Observation                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Risk   |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |

These observations are based on Coursera's published Privacy Notice.

### Privacy Risks Identified

• A large amount of personal data is collected, including date of birth, address, education and career details, and in some cases photo ID and biometric facial data.
• Session-replay tools and cookies can closely track user activity on the site.
• Personal data may be shared with many parties, including partner universities, content providers, instructors, employers, advertising contractors and government authorities.
• Learner progress and completion data may be shared with an employer or institution if the account is part of an Enterprise program.
• Forum posts, assignments and survey answers may contain personal information and may be published.
• Most data has no fixed retention period.
• Account deletion may be disabled for some users, certificates cannot be recovered after deletion, and residual data may remain in logs and databases.
• Personal data may be transferred to the United States and other countries with weaker privacy protection.
• The Ollie mobile app is not covered by this notice, so users must read a separate policy.

### Recommendations

• Users should avoid sharing unnecessary personal information, especially in forums, assignments and surveys.
• Review account Settings and use the Cookies Preference Center to turn off session recording and advertising tracking.
• Use the "Do Not Sell/Share" link in the website footer where available.
• Opt out of marketing emails and partner communications.
• Review the permissions of any connected third-party accounts, such as Facebook or Google login.
• Provide identity verification data only when it is required for a certificate or service.
• Download certificates before requesting account deletion.
• Read the separate privacy notice before using the Ollie app or any employer or university program linked to Coursera.
• Coursera should state clear retention periods for all data categories.

### Result
The Data Privacy Audit of Coursera was successfully performed. The audit identified its data collection, identity verification, usage, cookies and session recording, third-party sharing, AI use, security measures, retention, user rights and potential privacy risks.

### Conclusion
Coursera provides a detailed Privacy Notice with clear sections on legal bases, user rights, deletion, international transfers and cookie controls. However, it collects a wide range of personal data, shares it with many parties and offers limited retention details. Users should remain careful about the information they provide, the cookies they accept and the third-party and employer programs linked to their account.
